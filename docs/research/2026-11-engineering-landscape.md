# Verification, Not Execution, Limits AI Mechanical Engineers

> Research snapshot of 2026-11-14, written for [the engineering roadmap](../ENGINEERING-ROADMAP.md). What it says about
> μ's own repository describes commit `94fd2fd`, before the job layer, pairing and PLAN v6; the roadmap's §3 has the
> current baseline. Everything else is external research, with its gaps listed at the end.

Agents can already drive CAD, meshing and solver tools, but every measured study that separates "it ran" from "it is right" shows a large gap, and no commercial, open-source or professional-body source supports removing the human reviewer. The same Foam-Agent stack scores **88.2% on execution and 33.6% once the field must match a reference** ([CFDLLMBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20374)); the best FEABench agent wrote executable COMSOL calls 88% of the time and solved **zero** problems completely ([FEABench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06260)); frontier coding agents reached 9 of 50 strict passes on FEA-graded CAD briefs only after ten feedback attempts ([H-CCX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17448)). The systems that do well keep the language model away from solver decks and put deterministic gates around it: a schema-validated spec feeding human-written templates reached under 1% error on standard FEniCS cases ([arXiv 2606.10928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10928)). Beyond CAD, FEA and CFD, the picture is uneven. Rule-based DFM checking is mature and available as open source; should-cost has no independently validated accuracy better than roughly ±10% after calibration; the public standards needed for bolted-joint, margin and report conventions (NASA-STD-5020B, ECSS-E-HB-32-23A, ECSS-E-ST-32C) can be coded directly, while open libraries for lugs, Hertz contact, fits, VDI 2230 and 3D tolerance analysis are missing; and no published tool produces a loads report, stress margins and test predictions for a small UAV airframe. For μ (mu), the toolchain operated by Claude Code, the evidence says the next investment is a verification and provenance layer, not more solvers: typed result states, a cited and unit-checked formula library, a known-answer regression suite, run records, a margin ledger and a recorded independent human check. On that basis the agent is a credible junior engineer working from procedures under full review, and the evidence does not yet support a senior role.

## Agents reach 88% execution but only a third correctness

The research record on LLM agents for simulation is consistent on one point: executing a case is easy to measure and easy to pass, and correctness is neither. Foam-Agent 2.0 reports 88.2% success with Claude 3.5 Sonnet on 110 OpenFOAM cases, but "success" means the case ran, with physical accuracy checked only in a few qualitative studies ([Foam-Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18178)). CFDLLMBench re-scored the same framework with a correctness rule (NMSE to a reference field, 10% or less) and got 0.336 on basic and 0.250 on advanced cases ([arXiv 2509.20374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20374)). Its authors also found that o3-mini answers 92% of graduate CFD multiple-choice questions while the best score on executable Python CFD problems is about 14%, so knowledge is not practice. Baselines are not comparable across papers: MetaOpenFOAM scores 85% in its own paper, 55.5% in Foam-Agent's, 13.6% under the correctness rule and 6.2% in ChatCFD's ([arXiv 2407.21320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21320); [arXiv 2506.02019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02019)). On the structural side, FeaGPT reports 432 of 432 wing configurations converging in CalculiX but gives no error against any analytical or experimental reference ([FeaGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21993)), and VFEAgent reaches 100% Abaqus job execution on 15 cases with no result comparison ([VFEAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28978)).

The failure modes explain why. ALL-FEM documents silent changes to input parameters, plane strain used where plane stress was required (a result that looks right on a plot), and insufficient mesh refinement near small features ([ALL-FEM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21011)). In ChatCFD about 60% of semantic failures in runnable cases were wrong boundary conditions, initial conditions or initial fields, and only 40% and 30% of two literature cases had an accurate configuration even when 80% and 60% ran ([ChatCFD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02019)). In the CAD-plus-FEA setting, CADEngBench found that about **58% of executable programs violate at least one requirement**, and that 573 matched-FEA attempts failed at meshing or boundary-condition assignment while CalculiX itself failed in only 5 of 1,809 ([CADEngBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09296)). The solver is rarely the weak link; the set-up is.

Three design choices recur in the better results. First, restrict what the model writes. The constrained FEniCS interface lets the model only parse a prompt into a validated JSON spec and routes it to five human-written templates, achieving 0.1% error on a cantilever, 0.5% on a plate with a hole, 1% on a Lamé cylinder and 0.21% on Cook's membrane, with the validator rejecting under-specified prompts instead of inventing values ([arXiv 2606.10928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10928)). ChatCFD's structured tables of file dependencies, boundary-condition keywords and dimensions gave 82.1% execution and 68.12% physical fidelity over 315 cases ([arXiv 2506.02019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02019)). Second, use error-driven reviewer loops: removing the reviewer cut Foam-Agent's correctness-scored result from 0.336 to 0.200 ([arXiv 2509.20374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20374)), and FEA feedback in H-CCX lifted mean requirement pass from 38.8% to 60.5% over ten attempts, at about 68 minutes per brief ([arXiv 2605.17448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17448)). Third, measure rather than look. Executable geometric tests agreed with human judges at AUC 0.928, against about 0.66 for Chamfer distance, CLIP or a vision-model judge ([CADTests](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07807)), render feedback did not improve CADTestBench pass rates, and editing a supplied model passed 66.7% to 72.3% of tasks against 28% to 30% for generation from nothing ([arXiv 2608.09296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09296)).

General engineering reasoning is also short of review grade. On EngDesign, which grades designs by simulation, the best model passed 34.38% of 101 tasks and the mechanical-systems domain peaked at 28.57%; failures were dominated by domain-knowledge errors (31–33%) and constraint violations (25–36%) rather than arithmetic (6–9%), and iterating against the evaluator lifted o3 to nearly 60% ([EngDesign](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16204)). On closed-form calculations, EngChain's best final-answer accuracy was about 63%, with 59.1% of genuine step errors conceptual and 14.3% computational ([EngChain](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01650v1)). TPS-CalcBench found that a calculator tool raised the best model's exact-match rate from 52.4% to 57.1%, and that a formula reference sheet added 2.3 to 5.0 points, so supplying vetted formulas helps more than supplying arithmetic ([TPS-CalcBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17966)). Drawing reading sits around 71–77% on MechVQA with multi-view projection the weakest skill ([MechVQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30794)). The older DesignQA result is the clearest evidence on rule compliance: with the whole Formula SAE rulebook in context GPT-4o scored 0.885 F1 on rule retrieval, and with naive retrieval 0.186 ([DesignQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07917)); those are 2024-era models, so the direction matters more than the values.

## Commercial and open-source tools publish claims, not accuracy

Commercial activity in 2026 is large and almost entirely unmeasured. P-1 AI describes its agent "Archie" as operating at junior mechanical and electrical engineer level and announced a Series A led by NEA, with no benchmarks or review process disclosed ([press release](https://www.vcaonline.com/news/2026072916/engineering-ai-startup-p-1-ai-announces-its-series-a-financing-led-by-nea-adding-jeff-immelt-to-the-company-s-board/)). Synera raised $40M for an agent platform over 80+ CAD and simulation tools, again with no quantified customer results ([Synera](https://synera.io/press/synera-raises-40m-series-b-to-scale-agentic-ai-engineering-for-global-manufacturers)). Leo AI's headline 96% accuracy has no published test set ([Machine Design](https://www.machinedesign.com/automation-iiot/article/55330891/leo-ai-leo-ai-how-cad-aware-ai-is-changing-mechanical-design-and-engineering-workflows)), and DraftAid's own FAQ describes an 80–90% complete draft that a drafter finishes ([DraftAid](https://draftaid.io/faq/)). The incumbents shipped official surfaces: Autodesk Fusion MCP servers, PTC's Onshape FeatureScript MCP server, Ansys 2026 R1's Discovery Validation Agent and Mesh Agent, and SimScale's Engineering AI; none comes with accuracy data ([Digital Engineering 24/7 on Ansys](https://www.digitalengineering247.com/article/synopsys-launches-ansys-2026-r1); [SimScale](https://www.simscale.com/press/simscale-opens-engineering-ai-agents/)). I found no independent accuracy study for any of these products; Verdantix's survey rates trust and governance as significant open problems and sees agents as best suited to orchestrating workflows that already have structured validation ([Verdantix](https://www.verdantix.com/insights/blog/from-copilots-to-agents--how-ai-adoption-is-evolving-in-industrial-design-engineering)).

What the products do reveal is where vendors put the human. Their first agents are checkers, not doers (Ansys validates a set-up against best practice), CloudNC's CAM Assist lets the user inspect and edit each operation before commit ([CloudNC](https://www.cloudnc.com/cam-assist/mastercam)), SimScale's agent pauses to check its set-up and routes clarifying questions to a person while the review stays in CoLab where engineers decide ([SimScale blog](https://www.simscale.com/blog/agentic-engineering-design-review-simulation-workflows/)), and none documents automatic sign-off. Zoo's Zookeeper, the closest commercial analogue to μ, is an LLM tool loop over a scriptable kernel with execute, snapshot and mass-property tools, and publishes no benchmark ([Zoo](https://zoo.dev/research/zookeeper)).

The open-source ecosystem has the same shape with more transparent tool semantics. freecad-mcp is GUI-coupled ([GitHub](https://github.com/neka-nat/freecad-mcp)); build123d-mcp adds measure, validate, hole-finding and a STEP export gate and recommends measuring after key steps and checking volume change on edits ([GitHub](https://github.com/pzfreo/build123d-mcp)). FEP Agent Hub returns `BLOCKED` for unsupported capabilities instead of fabricating output and gates success on process, log, artefact and finite-value checks ([Glama listing](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/n65dgr8fqs)). text-to-cad carries a `dfm` command and deterministic measurement tools, but an independent test in a clean container passed 1 of 6 steps because of an undocumented missing system library ([andrew.ooo review](https://andrew.ooo/posts/text-to-cad-review-agent-cad-plugin/)), which is an argument for μ's rule that every change must pass its benchmark suite on a fresh build. Practitioner reports add hazards no benchmark captures: an OpenCASCADE `union()` that silently discarded a core cylinder at exact tangency with no error ([ModelRift via summary](https://zeli.app/story/49676577)), and a source-available hardware-in-the-loop project whose rules are that thresholds are never lowered, a test that exits without its summary line counts as a failure, and an engineer reviews before anything is ordered ([GitHub](https://github.com/Kemerd/claude-hardware-in-the-loop)). The ModelRift and Hacker News items were read through mirrors and aggregators, and the hardware-in-the-loop project's fuller lessons file could not be fetched.

## Rule-based DFM is mature; DFA and should-cost still need a human and a calibration

Manufacturability checking splits cleanly into what geometry can decide and what it cannot. The commercial checkers (DFMPro, DFMXpress, NX DFM Advisor) are parameterised ratios with editable thresholds shown as pass/fail lists on highlighted faces, and their vendor admits "under-report or over-report in exceptional cases" ([DFMPro FAQ, snippet only](https://dfmpro.geometricglobal.com/support/faqs-about-dfmpro/)). Numeric limits a team can cite come from service bureaus, and they disagree: Protolabs Network recommends hole depth of 4 × diameter with 10 × typical, while SendCutSend allows up to 8 × for small holes; machined wall minima run from 0.51 mm (Protolabs) to 0.8 mm recommended (Protolabs Network, JLCCNC) ([Hubs CNC guide](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-parts-cnc-machining/); [SendCutSend](https://sendcutsend.com/guidelines/cnc-machining/); [Protolabs](https://www.protolabs.com/services/cnc-machining/cnc-milling/design-guidelines/)). A rule base therefore needs recommended, feasible and shop-specific tiers, and a per-shop profile. The vendors that discuss robustness name B-rep topology variance as the hard part: Paperless Parts found one hole arriving as a single 360-degree face from one exporter and two 180-degree faces from another ([Paperless Parts](https://www.paperlessparts.com/blog/under-the-hood-our-philosophy-on-computational-geometry/)). Learned feature recognisers reach 97–99% on synthetic benchmarks, but real-part evidence is one public NIST part, qualitative, and the newest industrial paper needed 270 hand-labelled parts and released neither code nor data ([FeatureFox](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26770)).

LLM judgement alone over-flags. GPT-4V called all 20 test designs unprintable, including the 10 that printed successfully, and scored 0 of 60 on machining feature identification ([Picard et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.12668v3)); the best closed models in MUSE reach only about 19–21% on manufacturable, assemblable and functional criteria, scored by a VLM judge ([MUSE](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28579v1)). The defensible architecture is the one text-to-cad already follows: a deterministic `dfm` command emits findings with rule, value, limit, unit and source, and the agent may only cite them ([manufacturing-review reference](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/main/skills/cad/references/manufacturing-review.md)). Its output states "no findings is not a blanket manufacturability approval", separates NOT CHECKED from pass, and lists rules not evaluated, which is the honesty about coverage the rest of the field lacks. No published evaluation of an LLM-plus-kernel DFM reviewer against human manufacturing engineers exists, so seeded-defect parts and known-good parts, as Picard et al. used, are the only way to obtain a defect-miss and false-alarm rate for a team's own stack.

Design for assembly splits the same way. The Boothroyd-Dewhurst index is E_ma = 100 × N_min × t_a / t_ma, with t_a about 2.93 s ([open textbook](https://pressbooks.palni.org/designmanufactureassembly/chapter/chapter-6/)). Part count, fastener commonality, envelope size and thickness, symmetry, mass, interference, collision-free extraction directions and straight-line tool access are computable from a CAD assembly; the three minimum-part questions (relative motion in service, material necessity, service access), handling flags and insertion difficulty are not ([dfma.com](https://www.dfma.com/software/dfma-product-simplification.asp)). The handling and insertion time tables sit in the textbook, not in any open source found, and no maintained open OpenCascade tool for extraction-direction or blocking-graph analysis was identified. A workable design pre-fills the geometric columns and marks every functional answer "assumed, needs engineer confirmation".

Should-cost is where claims outrun evidence. The implementable model is material from purchasable stock plus setup amortised over batch plus cycle time × machine rate plus tooling, inspection and outside processes, and the hard input is cycle time ([CloudNC](https://www.cloudnc.com/blog/how-to-quote-cnc-machining-jobs)). The best vendor accuracy claim is ±10% "after calibration" ([3D Spark](https://www.3dspark.de/3d-spark-part-screening)); learned models report 3.91% to 18.51% test MAPE only within 24 narrow product groups using 13,684 historical drawings ([arXiv 2508.12440](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12440v1)); no public machine-rate dataset and no independent validation of process-based should-cost against real quotes was found. An uncalibrated estimate should be reported as low, likely and high with assumptions listed, and rates kept as configuration. Composite parts have a usable, checkable rule set in public aerospace guidance (symmetric balanced stacks, at most four grouped plies, ply drops at 20:1, edge distance 3 × diameter, pitch 4 × diameter, countersink at most 70% of thickness) that needs only a ply table and hole geometry ([Abbott AA-SB-001](https://abbottaerospace.com/?p=55173)). For additive work, build time and filament mass come from slicer G-code comments, and PySLM provides support volume and build time for metal powder bed fusion; no open distortion predictor was confirmed.

## Handbook calculations are codable from public standards but open libraries are thin

For the calculations a stress office expects, public sources are better than public software. NASA-STD-5020B (revalidated January 2025) gives preload bounds with relaxation and creep terms (Eq. 1–5), torque-to-preload scatter (25% lubricated and 35% unlubricated torque control, 10% for bolt stretch), the bolt-load and separation equations (Eq. 8–11), tension-shear-bending interaction, a 1.15 minimum fitting factor, and friction limits (no better than 0.20 clean uncoated, 0.10 for anything coated or lubricated) ([NASA-STD-5020B](https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/NASA/B/0/2021-08-06-nasa-std-5020b_final.pdf)). ECSS-E-HB-32-23A Rev.1 adds thread pull-out with explicit validity ranges, embedding allowances and a named margin-of-safety checklist that maps directly onto a report table ([ECSS handbook](https://ecss.nl/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/ECSS-E-HB-32-23A-Rev.1(6February2023).pdf)). VDI 2230's R0–R13 sequence is documented but the guideline is paywalled, and the GitHub implementations are small, GUI-bound and in one case self-described as "developed largely by using of LLM" ([GitHub](https://github.com/Come2World/VDI2230-Bolt-Checker)). For composite bolted joints the open options are bjsfm (last release 2022, Python 3.9 pin) and the new lamkit (0.1.4, 2026), both needing a validation set before use in a signed-off report ([PyPI bjsfm](https://pypi.org/project/bjsfm); [PyPI lamkit](https://pypi.org/project/lamkit/)). Bonded joints rest on Hart-Smith's public NASA reports, and NASA-STD-5001B applies an ultimate factor of 2.0 at composite discontinuities ([NASA-STD-5001B](https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/NASA/B-w/CHANGE-3/3/2022-10-24-NASA-STD-5001B-w-Change-3-Approved.pdf)).

Fatigue and tolerance work are better served. pyLife (Apache-2.0, 2.3.1, April 2026) includes rainflow counting, S-N and FKM nonlinear local-strain methods, and FLife (MIT, 3.0.1, November 2026) covers spectral vibration fatigue including Dirlik ([PyPI pylife](https://pypi.org/project/pylife/); [PyPI FLife](https://pypi.org/project/FLife/)). dimstack (MIT, 0.9.0) is the only maintained open 1D stack library with worst-case, RSS, modified RSS and six-sigma ([PyPI dimstack](https://pypi.org/project/dimstack)). No open 3D variation solver equivalent to CETOL, 3DCS or VSA was found, though a parametric code-CAD model can be sampled and rebuilt, which captures dimensional but not GD&T zone variation ([Sigmetrix](https://www.sigmetrix.com/meet-sigmetrix/product-faqs)). Service-bureau tolerances are capability bands, not Cpk data, so a stack must print the assumed conversion (for example band equals ±3σ). Machine elements are the weakest area: sectionproperties, PyNite, fluids and CoolProp are healthy, but obvious PyPI names for lugs, Hertz contact, ISO 286 fits and VDI bolts do not exist ([PyPI sectionproperties](https://pypi.org/project/sectionproperties/)), so these need an in-house formula library in which each function carries document, equation number and validity range.

GD&T and drawings are a split decision. Because the CAD is code, tolerances can be attached to features in the model script and written as semantic PMI, so the agent need not read GD&T from pixels, but OpenCASCADE's PMI path is thinly documented and its forum evidence shows full round-trip only for OCCT-written files ([OCCT XDE guide](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/occt_user_guides__xde.html); [OCCT forum](https://dev.opencascade.org/content/issue-reading-geometric-tolerance-values-nxnist-generated-step-ap242-files-occt-79)). cadtokens, a pure-Python Apache-2.0 reader validated on the 17 NIST AP242 files (362 of 362 geometric tolerances found, 360 linked to geometry), is a usable independent checker ([GitHub](https://github.com/Giltsch/cadtokens)). Headless drawings are moving fast: draftwright emits dimensioned multi-view drawings from a STEP file via CLI, but it is AGPL-3.0 and its Python line has stopped in favour of a Rust CLI, so invoking it as an external tool on exported STEP is the lower-risk use ([PyPI draftwright](https://pypi.org/project/draftwright/)). Review of GD&T by AI has no measured evidence: a 2026 review found none for mechanical GPS, GD&T, PMI or tolerance auditing (snippet only; [IJCIM](https://doi.org/10.1080/0951192X.2026.2714117)), and a practitioner view is that tools recognise syntax but cannot judge whether a datum scheme matches function ([CoLab blog, vendor-hosted](https://www.colabsoftware.com/post/why-most-engineering-drawing-ai-tools-fail-at-gd-t)). The defensible split is that the agent generates drawings from the model with rule-based dimensioning and the human owns the datum scheme.

Materials data is where provenance matters most. Aircraft regulations require statistically based design values (A-basis 99/95 for single-load-path members, B-basis 90/95 for redundant) ([14 CFR 25.613](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/25.613)); MMPDS is paid and NCAMP publishes free composite and printed-polymer allowables that each user must show equivalent to their own process ([NIAR NCAMP](https://www.wichita.edu/industry_and_defense/NIAR/Research/ncamp.php)). The pinned `bd_materials` package says it "is not a materials database", with every property a min–max range for early design ([PyPI bd_materials](https://pypi.org/project/bd_materials/)). A materials layer must therefore carry basis, source and revision, product form, direction (print orientation for FDM, where Z-built strength was 37–78% lower in one study ([Zohdi and Yang](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8512748))), environment and approval status, and refuse to compute a margin from a "typical" allowable without a loud flag. For presentation, handcalcs 1.11 renders ordinary Python as formula, substitution and result, and its v2 release candidate adds Markdown, HTML, ASCII and Word renderers that remove the Jupyter dependency, though it does not enforce units, which pint or forallpeople must supply ([PyPI handcalcs](https://pypi.org/project/handcalcs/)). The margin-of-safety convention is public: MS = allowable / (limit load × factor of safety) − 1 ([NASA-STD-5001B](https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/NASA/B-w/CHANGE-3/3/2022-10-24-NASA-STD-5001B-w-Change-3-Approved.pdf)).

## Credible simulation means standards-shaped evidence, and CalculiX has a known soft spot

The standards agree on a four-part skeleton: code verification, solution verification, validation, and uncertainty, all scoped to a stated use. NASA-STD-7009B is the most reusable for an automated pipeline because it is a list of records and warnings, not thresholds. It requires intended use, assumptions, input pedigree, a record of observed warning and error messages with explanations ("unexplained warning or error messages are a cause for concern"), a statement of uncertainty or an explicit statement that none is available, and mandatory placards when use leaves the verified and validated domain ([NASA-STD-7009B](https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/NASA/B/1/NASA-STD-7009B-Final-3-5-2024.pdf)). ASME V&V 40 scales evidence with model risk (model influence times decision consequence) ([Morrison et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6493688)), which maps onto how deeply a human checker should dig. The Celik et al. grid-convergence procedure supplies the arithmetic: three grids with refinement ratio above 1.3, observed order p, extrapolated value, and GCI with a 1.25 safety factor, with the warning that local order varied from 0.012 to 8.47 and 20% of points converged in oscillatory fashion even on a simple case ([Celik et al.](https://qa12.asme.org/wwwasmeorg/media/ResourceFiles/Shop/Journals/JFENumAccuracy.pdf)). ESRD's critique is that credibility scales show process maturity but do not quantify decision-specific prediction error, and that peak stress at a singularity is not a valid quantity of interest ([ESRD, vendor blog](https://www.esrd.com/?p=34333)).

CalculiX 2.23 is reliable where its author says it is, and weak where a UAV airframe lives. Its manual recommends quadratic elements, face-to-face penalty or mortar contact, a linearised run before any nonlinear one, and pretension sections ([CalculiX 2.23 manual](https://www.dhondt.de/ccx_2.23.pdf)). Shells and beams are expanded internally into solids with rigid "knots" at junctions; composite shells work only on S8R and S6, cannot report reaction forces in the `.frd` file, and cannot use the error estimators; the manual has no arc-length (Riks) or random-vibration step and the only fatigue capability is cyclic crack propagation ([same manual](https://www.dhondt.de/ccx_2.23.pdf)). Independent cross-solver evidence is thin and dated, but it is pointed: on the CoFEA hemispherical-shell test CalculiX shells were wrong by more than 99% while a quadratic solid model with three elements through the thickness was satisfactory, and code_aster passed with −0.5% ([CoFEA](https://cofea.readthedocs.io/en/latest/benchmarks/005-hemispherical-shell-point-load/results.html)). On NAFEMS LE10 CalculiX gave results from −1.51 to −7.34 MPa on coarse meshes and −3.08 to −5.77 MPa on fine ones against a −5.38 target depending on element type, with a "fine" quadratic tetrahedral result (−5.77) further off than the coarse one (−5.45) ([CoFEA LE10](https://cofea.readthedocs.io/en/latest/benchmarks/001-thick-plate/results.html)), so regression benchmarks must be convergence tests, not single-node tolerances. For a mostly thin-composite airframe, CalculiX shells are the largest single technical risk, and a code_aster spike is justified by one 2021 benchmark, not proven.

Meshing and singularities can be gated automatically. Gmsh exposes per-element quality by name (minSICN, minSJ, gamma) ([Gmsh 4.15.2 manual](https://gmsh.info/doc/texinfo/gmsh.html)); CalculiX has a gradient-based stress error indicator and `*REFINE MESH`, tested only on static procedures ([CalculiX forum](https://calculix.discourse.group/t/mesh-refinement/91)); and Sinclair and Beisheim's divergence checks correctly rejected all 265 stresses at 32 singularities (abstract level; [J. Appl. Mech.](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_crossref_primary_10_1115_1_4041766)). For CFD, the evidence for a small UAV is mixed. Multiple-reference-frame k-ω SST reproduced an APC 11×5.5E propeller within 0.43% to 10.60% on thrust and 4.6% to 7.9% on power against UIUC data ([SimScale validation](https://www.simscale.com/docs/validation-cases/drone-propeller-study/)), a bluff body stayed under 10% on force ([arXiv 2506.00305](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.00305)), but fully turbulent SST misses laminar separation at low Reynolds numbers, which pushes airfoil work to transition models or panel codes ([arXiv 2604.06913](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.06913)), and VSPAERO is inviscid with few public validation cases ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210017397/downloads/SheridanPham2021%200805.pdf)). The reasonable plan is to publish accuracy bands next to results, make the NASA TMR cases and the UIUC propeller database standing regression cases, and report numerical and input uncertainty separately from stated model-form limits. Industry practice for democratising simulation is the same pattern: experts author and validate locked templates, others vary inputs inside a declared envelope, and hard cases route back to experts ([engineering.com](https://www.engineering.com/democratizing-simulation-delivering-measurable-results/)); full SPDM platforms were called "an extreme disappointment" by CIMdata in 2019 ([Manufacturing.net](https://www.manufacturing.net/article/2019/05/democratization-simulation-and-analysis)), so the guardrails matter more than the platform.

## Checking, sign-off and responsible charge remain human by every available text

The design-assurance literature describes what a reviewable package looks like. NASA's gates ask for preliminary mechanical loads, stress, fracture and torque-margin analyses at PDR and completed ones at CDR, drawings above 80% complete at CDR, and closed TBD/TBR items ([GSFC-STD-1001A](https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/GSFC/A/0/gsfc-std-1001-a_extension_04-01-2020.pdf)); several of those items are computable from a repository. ECSS-E-ST-32C Annex K is the best public stress-report template: explicit units, a table of required factors of safety with sources, a table of allowables with basis (A, B or typical) and source, documented margin formulae, an explicit statement when no significant assumptions were made, and a "Minimum Margins of Safety Summary Table" ([ECSS-E-ST-32C](https://ecss.nl/wp-content/uploads/standards/ecss-e/ECSS-E-ST-32C31July2008.pdf)). On checking, Goddard's eight steps start with the purpose ("if he or she has not included an overview, perhaps you should consider rejecting the calculation"), then assumptions, references, drawings, method, independent replication, credible failure modes and reasonableness ([Goddard 2000](https://www.varmintal.com/HOW2CHEK006.pdf)). One contractor manual mirror adds that the verifier must be independent of the design, should use different tools from the originator, and lists "using the originator's spreadsheet or software" and "following the originator's sequence" as pitfalls ([unofficial mirror](https://kbr-manual.pages.dev/technical/verification)). Reading these together, a second Claude instance sharing prompts, tools and inputs would not satisfy the spirit of independent verification; that conclusion is an inference, not a statement from any source.

Professional regulators are unambiguous and consistent. EGBC's advisory, adopted by PEO, says professionals remain responsible for AI-generated work, must not rely on AI outputs for safety-related projects unless they understand the reasoning, must record tool make and version and validate with known test cases, and need independent review by a separate professional for high-risk work ([EGBC via PEO](https://www.peo.on.ca/sites/default/files/2025-11/EGBC_Use_of_Artificial_Intelligence_in_Professional_Work.pdf)). North Carolina requires the licensee to be "capable of reproducing the output independently from any AI program" ([NCBELS](https://www.ncbels.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Guidelines-on-the-Use-of-Artificial-Intelligence-in-Engineering-and-Surveying-Services-FINAL.pdf)); Newfoundland and Labrador requires outputs be independently verified "using traditional methods or professional judgment" with traceable references to tools, sources and verification steps ([PEGNL](https://pegnl.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/PEGNL-Practice-Guideline-for-the-Use-of-AI-V6.pdf)); and Engineers Europe states that sign-off responsibility "is personal and cannot be delegated to tools or systems", with high-risk uses needing formal verification, traceability and independent review ([Engineers Europe](https://engineerseurope.com/sites/default/files/2026-07/Advice_Note_on_AI.pdf)). EASA's AI work targets machine-learning systems in aircraft and defers generative AI ([EASA NPA 2025-07](https://www.easa.europa.eu/newsroom-and-events/news/easas-first-regulatory-proposal-artificial-intelligence-aviation-now-open)), so it informs vocabulary but does not constrain an engineering design tool today. By analogy with DO-330 tool qualification (a framework not written for this case), independent human verification of every output makes tool qualification unnecessary, and sampled or reduced checking would amount to claiming verification credit for the agent, which requires evidence of its reliability ([Rapita DO-330 overview, vendor](https://www.rapitasystems.com/do-330)).

The evidence on human review of AI output argues for designing the reviewer's task carefully. In a controlled experiment with 29 professional developers, AI-assisted review found more low-severity issues but no more high-severity ones, took about 56 minutes against 42 manually (not statistically significant), and narrowed coverage from 484 to 371–407 distinct lines, which the authors attribute to bias from seeing the automated review first ([Tufano et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.11401v3)). Cognitive-forcing designs reduce over-reliance but are rated less favourably ([Buçinca et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692)), and an assumption-first intervention reduced over-reliance without added load ([Ghosh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18033)). No study of engineers reviewing AI-generated FE or calculation packages was found. The practical implications are to have the checker record an independent estimate before seeing the agent's margin, make them disposition each assumption, show where the agent deviated from procedure, and seed known errors in a small fraction of packages, the only method in these studies that measures detection rather than activity.

Adjacent processes have usable open mechanisms. Doorstop stores requirements as YAML with a SHA-256 review fingerprint, so a change to an item or its parent makes the link stale ([Doorstop](https://doorstop.readthedocs.io/en/latest/reference/item.html)); StrictDoc and sphinx-needs offer richer exchange and document integration ([StrictDoc](https://strictdoc.readthedocs.io/en/stable/stable/docs/strictdoc_01_user_guide.html); [sphinx-needs](https://sphinx-needs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)). The Process Run Crate profile of Workflow Run RO-Crate already defines an execution record with instrument, software version, inputs, outputs, agent, times, status and container image ([Process Run Crate](https://www.researchobject.org/workflow-run-crate/profiles/process_run_crate/)), whose `agent` field is where AI authorship can be recorded honestly. LLM-generated FMEA evidence is weak and old: IBM's study on 2023–24 open models measured recall of 0.559 to 0.573 on its first two FMEA sections ([Lynch et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05054v1)), a design-science study found 80–91% semantic agreement on failure modes extracted from customer reviews but "no quantitative analysis" of proposed severity, occurrence and detection numbers ([El Hassani et al.](https://doi.org/10.1017/dsj.2025.7)), and no study measured recall of mechanical design failure modes against an expert team. Agents can draft structure and function analysis and propose failure modes; humans must own severity and detection ratings. For autonomy levels, NAFEMS PSE defines Entry (supervised with clear guidelines), Standard (independent on well-defined procedures, aware of own limits) and Advanced (plans analysis strategy and validation) ([NAFEMS](https://nafems.org/professional-development/certification/about/levels)), and an academic framework treats autonomy as a design choice separate from capability, with a certified maximum level per agent specification ([Feng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12469v2)). No source applies either ladder to an AI agent; the mapping is mine.

## Small UAV airframe substantiation: criteria exist, the open toolchain for them does not

The only freely readable numeric structural rule set for the sub-150 kg class is AEP-83, the NATO STANAG 4703 text: limit factor of safety at least 1.0, ultimate at least 1.5 for structure whose failure is hazardous or worse and 1.25 otherwise, special factors (2.0 castings, 1.15 fittings, 1.2 or 1.5 for composites lacking hot-wet statistics), manoeuvre factors +3.8 and −1.5, gusts of 15.2 and 7.6 m/s, flutter freedom to 1.2 VD, and composite damage-tolerance strain limits of 5000 microstrain in tension and 2600–3000 in compression ([AEP-83 Ed A v1](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7ec5c940f0b62305b83155/20140916-STANAG-4703_AEP-83_A__1_.pdf)). That is the 2014 edition and covers fixed wing; the 2023 edition is paywalled and was not read. For multirotors, EASA's proposed SAIL III means of compliance defines the manoeuvre factor from thrust, n_max = k × (total maximum thrust)/(minimum weight) with n_min = −1, and requires a hot structural test at 54 °C or an extra 1.25 factor; the coefficient k appears as 2.0 or 1.5 in a redlined text and must be read from the final document ([EASA MoC OSO#2, proposed](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/downloads/142271/en)). Indian civil certification, per the QCI FAQ, treats static test to limit load as the basic requirement, accepts theoretical calculation for small classes, and expects FEA reports to show mesh, boundary conditions and materials with a 1.5 factor ([QCI FAQ v3](https://padd.qci.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/QCI_UAS_FAQs_V3_23_Apr_2024.pdf)). All frameworks converge on one evidence chain: declared load cases, analysis with stated allowables, static test of primary structure, flight-test vibration evidence, and a life or inspection statement.

The analysis set a reviewer would expect is long, and the research synthesised 24 items, from the compliance matrix and mass-properties ledger through multirotor and winged load cases, composite and printed-part strength, bolted and bonded joints, buckling, modal and Campbell checks, vibration fatigue, flutter, propulsion, thermal, environmental and galvanic design, mechanical FMEA, and test plans with pre-test predictions. A few specifics make the point. Multirotor loads need at least a dozen cases that no standard enumerates (maximum differential thrust, yaw, one-motor-out, gyroscopic, imbalance, landing at 9–18 inch drop heights, payload inertial loads); a Campbell-style check against 1/rev and blade-pass lines is practice but no numeric separation rule exists in any source found; ArduPilot's 30 m/s² IMU vibration threshold is the one concrete acceptance number ([ArduPilot](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/common-measuring-vibration.html)); and random-vibration fatigue would need frequency-response post-processing outside CalculiX. Published multirotor frame papers rarely combine a regulatory load definition, composite failure criteria, joint analysis, modal placement and test correlation, and achieved analysis-to-test agreement in the cited studies is 1.9% (composite wing ultimate load, paper-record level) to 13% (nylon bracket) ([DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/ea4cb425f3aa473296ad2a700713f21a)). Open frameworks stop at conceptual sizing and aero (SUAVE, Aviary, OpenVSP, DUST), and LLM work on UAVs is at configuration level ([arXiv 2504.08981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08981)); no public work was found on an agent producing a certification-style structural report. A set of CLI analyses emitting loads, margins and test predictions would be new relative to everything found, but that is also a statement that nobody has measured how well it works.

## What this implies for extending μ

μ today is a reproducible installer and version manager around third-party tools with about 450 lines of its own engineering code, per the internal review of the repository. Its FEA driver handles linear static and modal analysis on second-order tetrahedra with isotropic materials and bonded joints only, computes its margin of safety from the raw peak integration-point stress (the repository's own legacy policy says not to trust such peaks), applies no singularity handling, and returns no run record. The deepest capability, the vendored text-to-cad DFM, interference and kinematics checks, is not benchmarked or mentioned in μ's own instructions, so a pin bump could change what "OK" means unnoticed. Most of the planned Claude Code layer (run records, a `check` command, hooks, report skills, the org profile) is absent, and the six-rule validation policy exists only in a repository the review could not see. These facts matter because the evidence above says that exactly this layer, not additional solvers, separates a helpful assistant from a hazard.

The evidence supports a build order. First, repair the trust defects in what exists: the margin from raw peak stress, the load leakage between static steps, point masses excluded from gravity, silent partial overlaps and the swapped-shear principal stress field, and add benchmarks for the unexercised paths (modal, multi-part, `summarize`, `dfm`), since a gate that does not exercise a feature cannot protect it. Second, give every analysis command a result schema with distinct fields for executed, converged, checks passed, verified-against (reference case or requirement ID) and unverified, so a report generator refuses to print "verified" over an empty reference; this is the lesson of the 88% versus 33.6% gap, and text-to-cad's `ok / failed / not_checked / usage` statuses with exit codes 0, 1, 3, 2 are a convention to match. Third, write run records mapped to the Process Run Crate terms and block any reported number that has no record. Fourth, move from freehand deck writing toward a typed analysis spec with named selections, a deterministic generator, pre-solve gates (unit and dimension check, every selection resolves, rigid-body-motion check, load-resultant echo, material from the curated table, mesh quality) and post-solve gates (reaction balance, mass against CAD, effective modal mass, three-grid GCI with observed order and an oscillation warning, singularity classification, a stiffness sensitivity run for contact, and parsing of solver warnings so a pass is blocked while any is unexplained). Fifth, build the cited formula library on NASA-STD-5020B, ECSS, NASA-STD-5001B and the public composite and bonded-joint sources, with unit-carrying quantities, validity-range assertions and regression tests drawn from the standards' own worked examples; this exploits the TPS-CalcBench finding that vetted formulas help more than a calculator, and its residual error rate in combination with units and code execution is unmeasured and must be measured internally. Sixth, carry a materials table with basis, source and direction that refuses margins on typical values.

Sequence matters for what the agent may do and when. The benchmark suite should be tiered: the CalculiX distribution's reference files on every build, closed-form and NAFEMS-style cases through μ's own driver written as convergence tests, and feature benchmarks added as each capability lands (Hertz for contact, a shell and composite set, a bolted joint against hand calculation, a UIUC propeller for CFD). A shell benchmark set must pass before any composite skin result is called anything but unverified, and a second-solver spike should test code_aster on thin shells. For the UAV, the first deliverables should be the ones where hand methods exist and reviewers expect them: a design-basis document with an adopted and cited factor set (AEP-83 factors plus the EASA thrust-based multirotor load factor, with the hot-wet knock-downs as named items), a loads module that generates the multirotor case set from vehicle parameters with an equilibrium check, margin tables per primary structural element in ECSS Annex K form, a mass-properties ledger with maturity codes and a CG envelope, a Campbell check, and pre-test predictions with a correlation table. Those reports should put the bad news first, in the way NASA-STD-7009B asks: placards, unexplained warnings, unverified items and assumptions before results, with a minimum-margin table and a coverage statement of what was not evaluated. Requirements belong in files with review fingerprints, and a mechanical FMEA should be drafted as every part crossed with a standard failure-mode list and every load case, with "not applicable because" entries, because omission, not invention, is the measured weakness.

For the human side, the toolchain should record the verification act as a first-class object: who checked, by which independent method, against which input and output hashes, with their own estimate logged before seeing the agent's margin. This is the piece the regulators' texts require and the one most toolchains lack. A workable autonomy ladder for the agent has three levels, drawn from NAFEMS PSE and UK-SPEC wording: an entry level executing only from a human-written analysis plan with every package fully checked; a standard level selecting among approved methods, running its own V&V per policy, flagging novelty as an explicit applicability check, with full checks on safety-critical items and risk-weighted sampling elsewhere; and an advanced level proposing strategy and validation tests, still without removing independent verification. Promotion should be per technical area and based on measured evidence at the pinned toolchain version (benchmark pass rates, checker finding rates and severity over a set of packages, seeded-error detection), and any change of model, prompts or tools should reset it. Anything that could be said about a "senior" role is unsupported by the evidence found; "junior with full review, moving toward standard for well-defined procedures" is the claim that can be defended.

## Limitations and gaps

The shared web-search budget ran out part-way through the research for several of the notes, so later material was gathered by fetching known URLs, and topics such as Fusion manufacturability tools, Materialise and Oqton, distortion prediction, code_aster and Elmer maturity, open-source UQ tooling, the S-120A mass growth table and many standards texts were left as gaps rather than filled from memory. Where a source is described above as snippet-only or aggregator-read, the claim has not been checked against the primary page; vendor figures (P-1 AI, Synera, Leo AI, DraftAid, CloudNC, 3D Spark, SimScale) are labelled as vendor claims and none has independent verification. Many measured results use 2024–2025 models (DesignQA, EngDesign, the FMEA studies), so they indicate direction and failure types, not current capability. Several baselines cannot be compared across papers because each uses its own benchmark. The evidence is thin or absent on benchmarks for tolerance stack-up, fastener sizing and fatigue, on CalculiX results against NAFEMS cases with agents, on engineers reviewing AI-generated analysis, on the benefit of CI for mechanical CAD, and on regulator treatment of autonomous agents rather than chat assistants. AEP-83 numbers are from the 2014 edition, ASTM F3298 and F3657 were paywalled, the EASA multirotor coefficients were read from a redlined proposed text, and the CoFEA results date from about 2021 and predate CalculiX 2.23. The repository review is a read-only inspection that did not run the toolchain; its inferred items (for example the principal-stress shear order) rest on documented file formats.

## Conclusion

The central finding is an asymmetry. Agents are now good enough at the producing half of mechanical analysis (writing code, driving mesh and solver tools, assembling tables) that execution is no longer the bottleneck, and every measured study shows that correctness, and above all the quality of the set-up, is. That moves the value of an engineering toolchain from capability to evidence: what makes an agent's report review-ready is not a larger analysis menu but result states that cannot say "verified" without a reference, numbers that cannot appear without a run record, formulas that cannot be chosen from outside a cited library, and a reviewer task designed against anchoring. A toolchain that builds this layer first also wins on the audit side, since the regulators' requirements (tool version, inputs, outputs, test cases, independent verification) are things a repository can record automatically; the one item they require that nothing records yet is the human's own independent check.

Two opportunities follow that no surveyed source occupies. A benchmark that scores manufacturability by a geometric kernel instead of a vision judge, and an internal known-answer suite plus seeded-defect packages, would give the team the only accuracy numbers it can trust for its own stack, since cross-paper numbers for the same system disagree by up to an order of magnitude (MetaOpenFOAM: 85% in its own paper, 6.2% in a competitor's). And a small-UAV substantiation workflow with loads, margins and test predictions does not exist in open form, so the first version would exceed typical published practice in scope while being unmeasured in reliability; its honest claim is that it produces a package a competent engineer can verify quickly, which is exactly what the evidence says to test next.
